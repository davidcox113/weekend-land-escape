Instruction manual

How to Play

Everything you need to play Weekend Land Escape Wave 1: card types, the turn, combat, fortifications and the gold economy.

Draft / playtest rules, updated for Keywords v2. Creature keywords are now a set of 12 (see Keywords), and any unblocked creature attacking a fort deals 1 damage to it (Siege: full power). Expect wording, costs and timing to shift as Wave 1 locks. A card’s own text always wins if wording differs.

1.Overview

Weekend Land Escape is a fantasy kingdom trading card game for 2 players. Each player represents a warband fighting to hold territory, raise gold, raise walls and cut the enemy down. The baseline is a classic two-player trading card game, with Weekend Land Escape’s own gold economy, fortifications, equipment upkeep and Supply pressure.

GoalReduce your opponent to 0 life. Both players begin at 20 life. The first player to drop an opponent to 0 (or less) wins.

No dice required. Track life, gold, damage and counters with Treasure tokens, spare counters, a scoresheet or a phone. Combat math is power vs. toughness — no random rolls.

Multiplayer (optional)

In free-for-all, each player still starts at 20 life; the last player above 0 wins. Attacks target one opponent or their creatures as usual, and the shared Treasure pile sits in the middle. For two-headed / team play, a shared life total of 30 and a shared gold pool per team is a solid playtest default.

2.Mana Marks

Mana is produced by Lands and mana-producing Artifacts, and spent to cast spells and activate abilities. Weekend Land Escape uses six custom mana marks, locked for Wave 1:

Bastion (white / order)Defense, discipline, healing and righteous steel.
Harbor (blue / tempo)Draw, dig, bounce and battlefield timing.
Mire (black / removal)Attrition, taxes and hard answers.
Forge (red / aggression)Rush, pressure and warbands.
Wildwood (green / ramp)Growth, mana acceleration and big threats.
Quarry (colorless / structures)Artifacts, mana rocks, forts and walls.

3.Card Types

Every permanent and spell belongs to one or more types. Wave 1 ships creatures (including dragon mythics), lands, fortifications, economy buildings, equipment, mana rocks and Treasure tokens. Instants and sorceries are designed into the rules but are not in the Wave 1 card set yet.

Lands (Basics + Hybrids)

Lands produce mana. You may play one land per turn during a main phase (unless a card says otherwise). Basic lands tap for one mana of their mark. Hybrid lands tap for either listed mark — Crossroads Quarry is the special any-color/Quarry rare and enters tapped.

Creatures

Creatures have power / toughness. Power is the damage they deal in combat; toughness is their hit points. Creatures enter tapped only if a card says so; most cannot attack the turn they enter unless they have Aggressive (or a similar exemption). Dragon mythics are high-cost apex creatures — see Dragon Wave 1.

Instants & Sorceries (planned)

Instants can be cast any time you have priority — including during combat and on an opponent’s turn. Sorceries are cast only during your main phase when nothing else is waiting to resolve. Wave 1 does not include Instants or Sorceries yet; they are planned for later waves.

Artifacts

Wave 1 artifacts are primarily mana rocks (tap for colored or Quarry mana) and Equipment (see below). Artifacts are usually colorless to cast unless their cost lists a colored mark. Relics such as Dualheart Vessel support dragon synergies.

Fortifications

Structures use DEFENSE instead of power/toughness. When a fortification has taken damage equal to its DEFENSE, it is destroyed. Forts protect troops and economic buildings — walls reduce incoming pressure, towers shoot attackers, gates soak hits meant for markets. Any unblocked creature attacking a fort chips it for 1 damage; Siege creatures hit it for their full power.

Economy Fortifications

A subset of forts that produce Income (gold) during your upkeep. Richer buildings often have UPKEEP gold costs. Miss an upkeep and you typically forfeit that building’s income for the turn (see individual cards). No per-soldier wages — the economy is building-driven.

Equipment

Pay the cast cost to put Equipment onto the battlefield unattached. Pay its Equip cost (often gold) to attach it to a creature you control. Some heavy pieces also demand Equipment UPKEEP each turn — pay it or unequip.

Treasure Tokens

Each Treasure represents +1 gold. They are shared table components, not deck cards. When you spend gold, return that many Treasures to the shared pile.

4.Zones

  • Library — your shuffled deck, face down.
  • Hand — private cards you can cast or play (lands).
  • Battlefield — lands, creatures, forts, artifacts and equipment in play.
  • Graveyard — discarded, destroyed and resolved spells (face up).
  • Exile — removed from the game; only returns if a card says so.
  • Gold pile / Treasure pool — shared components beside the board. Your personal gold is the Treasures in front of you.

5.Deck Construction

Wave 1 playtest default (draft suggestion):

  • 40–60 cards. For a 40-card deck, aim for about 24 lands (scale proportionally for 60).
  • Maximum 4 copies of any non-basic card. Basic lands are unlimited.
  • Gold/Treasure tokens are not deck cards — keep a shared pile of 20–40 tokens for the table.

Color discipline matters. Two-mark decks are the sweet spot for early playtests; splash a third only if your hybrid lands and mana rocks support it. Dragon mythics are late-game finishers — build ramp, gold engines and tutors (e.g. Harbinger Whisker) before slamming a Colossus.

6.Setup

  1. Shuffle your library.
  2. Draw 7 cards.
  3. Optional mulligan: shuffle your hand back and draw 6 (one free mulligan to 6 is the Wave 1 default; further mulligans are your playgroup’s choice).
  4. Set life to 20.
  5. Set gold to 0 (no Treasures in front of you).
  6. Place the shared Treasure pile beside the board.
  7. Determine the first player randomly; that player skips the first draw step (recommended playtest rule).

7.Turn Structure

Each turn proceeds through these steps in order. For Wave 1 creature and fort play, resolve actions in turn order unless a card says otherwise (a fuller priority system arrives with Instants).

  1. Untap. Untap all permanents you control.
  2. Upkeep. In this order (recommended):
    • Collect Income from each Economy fortification you control (take that many Treasures from the pile).
    • Pay building UPKEEP for each Economy fort that demands it. If you cannot (or choose not to), follow the card — typically you get no income from that building this turn, or it becomes inactive.
    • Pay Equipment UPKEEP for equipped pieces that list one. If unpaid, unequip (the Equipment stays on the battlefield unattached).
    • Optional Supply check: if you control 5 or more creatures, you may pay 1 gold. If you don’t, all your creatures get −1/−0 until your next turn. There are no per-soldier wages — Supply is the only army-size tax.
  3. Draw. Draw one card (except the first player on turn one).
  4. Main Phase 1. Play a land (once per turn). Cast creatures, artifacts, fortifications and (later) sorceries. Equip creatures. Activate abilities.
  5. Combat. Declare attackers → declare blockers → damage. Fortification abilities may trigger or activate here (towers shoot, walls reduce damage, berms buff toughness).
  6. Main Phase 2. Same options as Main Phase 1 (still only one land per turn in total).
  7. End / Cleanup. “Until end of turn” effects wear off. Discard down to the maximum hand size of 7. Damage on creatures and forts wears off unless a card says it marks permanent damage.

8.Combat & Fortifications

Creatures deal combat damage equal to their power. A creature that has taken damage greater than or equal to its toughness is destroyed and goes to the graveyard. When multiple creatures block, the attacker’s controller chooses how to assign damage among the blockers (Wave 1 default), unless a keyword says otherwise.

Fortifications have DEFENSE instead of P/T. Damage assigned to a fortification accumulates for the turn; when total damage ≥ DEFENSE, the fortification is destroyed. Many forts redirect or reduce damage that would hit you, your creatures or your Markets.

Earthen Berm (1 Quarry, DEF 3) gives your creatures +0/+1 and protects gold from Instant-speed taxes once those cards exist — a cheap frontline berm for an early economy.

Ballista Tower (4 Forge + 1 Quarry, DEF 5) can shoot an attacker for 2 and grants economy forts +2 DEF, turning your markets into harder targets.

Attacking forts & SiegeWhen you attack, each attacking creature attacks the defending player or one of their Fortifications. If a creature attacking a fort is unblocked, it deals 1 damage to that fort. A creature with Siege deals its full power instead. Blocked attackers fight their blockers as usual. A fort is destroyed when the damage on it this turn is equal to or more than its DEFENSE. If a fort’s own text disagrees (for example, Thousand-Banner Citadel: only Siege creatures can damage it), the card wins.

Example: Ironbelly Siege-Hurler (3/4, Siege) and Warpaint Marauder (2/2) both attack your opponent’s Stone Curtain Wall (DEF 6). Nobody blocks. The Hurler deals 3 and the Marauder deals 1: 4 damage, so the Wall stands this turn. Add a second Siege creature, or a Fire Breath creature arriving before combat, and it falls.

9.Gold Economy

Gold is the second resource alongside mana. Track it with Treasure tokens (+1 gold each). When you gain gold, take Treasures from the shared pile. When you spend gold, return that many Treasures to the pile.

Inflows
Economy fort Income during upkeep; enters-the-battlefield bursts (e.g. War Chest); other card effects.
Outflows
Cast costs that list gold; building UPKEEP; Equip costs; equipment UPKEEP; optional Supply payment; dragon tribute / mythic gold costs.

Gold does not untap or reset — unspent Treasures stay in front of you between turns. Managing upkeep vs. board presence is a core Wave 1 skill. Dragon casting costs often demand 4–8 gold on top of heavy colored mana — plan markets and War Chests early.

10.Keywords

Keywords v2: every creature keyword in the game is one of these 12, two per mana mark. A card carries the keywords of its own marks. Cards with one keyword print this reminder text; cards with two keywords print them bare. Mythics may also have one named ability, explained on the card. Other rules words (Legendary, Equip, Income, Upkeep, Upgrade, Garrison, Token) work as described on this page.

Bastion

Armored
Prevent 1 damage each time this is dealt damage.
Rally
When this attacks, your other attacking creatures get +1/+0 this turn.

Harbor

Flying
Can only be blocked by creatures with Flying.
Snare
When this attacks, tap target creature with less power.

Mire

Relentless
When this dies, you may pay 1 gold to return it to your hand.
Lifedrink
When this deals combat damage, you gain that much life.

Forge

Aggressive
Can attack the turn it arrives.
Fire Breath X
When this enters, deal X damage to target creature or fort.

Wildwood

Pack
+1/+0 while attacking alongside another creature.
Camouflage
Can't be blocked by creatures with greater power.

Quarry

Charge
+2/+0 when attacking.
Siege
Deals its full power to a fort it attacks. (Without Siege, an unblocked attacker deals just 1 damage to a fort.)

Some cards shown in the gallery still carry their old printed keywords while their reprint is on the way. Their tile says “Updated rules text; reprint coming”: play them by the text on the website.

11.Card Format

Cards use a landscape layout with a side panel — art on the left or main area, text panel on the right. Physical cards are standard trading-card size (63 × 88 mm / 2.5 × 3.5 in), turned sideways: each card prints as a 3.5 in wide × 2.5 in tall landscape card. Use the card-size Treasure token on cut sheets; square chips and coins are deluxe extras. See Print & Play.

12.Worked Example Turn

The situationYou are on turn four. Life 18, gold 1 (one Treasure). You control Forge Peak, Bastion Fields, an Earthen Berm and a Warpaint Marauder (1 Forge, 2/2) you cast last turn. Your hand includes Camp Market and Militia Spear. Your opponent has a Wooden Palisade and no untapped creatures.
  1. Untap / Upkeep. Untap your lands, the Berm and the Marauder. No economy income yet. No equipment upkeep. You have 1 creature, so Supply does not apply.
  2. Draw. You draw Ironfang Bruiser — keep it for later.
  3. Main Phase 1. Play your land for the turn — a second Bastion Fields. Tap it to cast Camp Market (1 Bastion). Tap Forge Peak to cast Militia Spear (1 Forge), then Equip it onto the Marauder for 1 Gold, returning your Treasure to the pile. The Marauder is now 3/3 (2/2 base, Spear +1/+0, Berm +0/+1).
  4. Combat. The Marauder has been under your control since the start of the turn, so it can attack. Attack the player: unblocked, it deals 3 and your opponent drops from 20 to 17. (Attacking the Palisade instead would deal only 1 damage to it, because the Marauder doesn’t have Siege.)
  5. Main Phase 2 / End. Nothing else. Next upkeep, Camp Market will bring in +1 Treasure. Hold the line.

13.Dragon Wave 1 Draft

The Dragon Wave 1 section is a draft mythic package with original Weekend Land Escape names. Costs sit at the top of the ladder: multi-mark mana plus significant gold, with Tidecoil Colossus as the apex.

  • Mythic costs. Expect 4–8 gold plus 6–12 colored mana. Build your economy before you can cast them.
  • Siege vs. forts. Dragons with Siege (Worldcoil Sovereign, Platebound Warwyrm, Platebound — Hordemaster, or any Dragon wearing Adamantine Drakeplate) hit a fort for their full power, the answer to tall fort walls.
  • Alternates are either/or draft choices for the same slot unless a card notes otherwise. Example: take Bleakcloud Devourer or Bleakcloud — Divide, not both. Same idea for Platebound Warwyrm vs. Platebound — Hordemaster.
  • Support pieces. Harbinger Whisker fetches the ocean apex pair; Dualheart Vessel protects Ragbound Matriarch; Drakeplate gives a Dragon Siege.
Mini example — late gameYou have 8 gold, Harbor/Mire lands untapped, and Harbinger Whisker in play. Sacrifice Whisker and pay 3 gold to fetch Tidecoil Colossus, then cast it next turn after another Income cycle. When it attacks, Snare taps a smaller blocker and Crushing Tide deals 3 damage to each defending creature.
