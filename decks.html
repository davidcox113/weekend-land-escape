Playing with 100-Card DecksProposed

Proposed — not locked yet. These deck-building rules are a proposal for David and Tommy's 100-card games. They aren't official until David locks them, and they may change.

With 254 cards in the gallery (248 deck cards plus 6 tokens), there's now enough to build bigger decks. A 100-card deck plays mostly like a 60-card one. You see a wider mix of cards from game to game, and with only 2 copies of each card, no single card turns up every game.

Deck-building rules (proposed)

  • Proposed Exactly 100 cards.
  • Proposed Up to 2 copies of any non-land card.
  • Proposed Legendary cards: 1 copy.
  • Proposed Basic lands (Bastion Fields, Harbor Coast, Mire Hollow, Forge Peak, Wildwood Grove, Quarry Stone): unlimited.
  • Proposed Tokens are not put in the deck. Keep them beside the table, like Treasure.

Not decided yet: how many copies of a non-basic land (hybrids, tri-lands) you can play. Until David decides, the Deck Builder and the starter decks below allow 2. Alternate versions that share one Legendary slot (the three Count Valdreks, as their card text says, and the Bleakcloud and Platebound dragon alternates, per the Dragon rules) are 1 copy in total.

Starting life and gold: nothing new is proposed here. See Proposed balance changes: change 10 suggests 30 life for 100-card games, and change 1 (Land Tithe) would let every deck make its first gold.

Deck-building tips

  • About 40% lands works best: 24 in a 60-card deck, 40 in a 100-card deck. At 60% lands you flood badly, drawing about 8 lands by turn 8 when you need 5 or 6.
  • Watch your colored symbols. Every mana symbol on a card needs that exact kind of mana. A card that costs "3 Quarry" needs three Quarry-producing lands, not just three lands. Count your sources: if a card needs 3 of one mark, you want 10+ lands (or rocks) that make it.
  • Plan your first gold. Every player starts with 0 gold, and only a few cards make gold from nothing: Camp Market and Deepvein Miner today. If your deck has gold costs (lords, Equip, Dragons), play 4+ of these, or you may never cast them.
  • Build a curve first, then an economy. Economy buildings are great support, but a deck that's mostly buildings loses to one that's mostly creatures.
  • Dragons are a late-game treat. Today you'll only cast one in roughly 1 game in 6 by turn 10. Pack ramp, and have a plan to survive until then.
  • Bigger decks need more gold starters. A 100-card deck needs about three times as many gold starters as a 60-card deck to find one early just as often.

4. Proposed balance changesProposed

Proposed — not official. Nothing in this section is part of the rules yet. The designer hasn't locked any of these changes; keep playing with the current rules until an update is announced.

These are the changes our playtesting points to, roughly in order of how much they'd help. Nothing below is official, so keep playing with the current rules until an update is announced. If you try any of these at your table, we'd love to hear how it went.

  1. Proposed Land Tithe (proposed rule). Once per turn, you may tap one of your lands for 1 Gold instead of mana. Why: right now, more than half of non-Bastion decks have no gold at all by turn 6, so their gold-cost cards sit in hand. With this rule that drops to almost zero.
  2. Proposed Easier Dragon costs (proposed). Most of a Dragon's cost becomes "any mana", and the biggest Dragons come down from 12 to 10 mana and from 8 to 6 gold. Why: at the moment the 12-mana Dragons basically never get cast, and even the cheapest one arrives by turn 10 only about 1 game in 6. The same idea would apply to other cards with a long row of one symbol, like Kingshold Keep.
  3. Proposed Forts can block (proposed rule). Each non-economy Fortification may block one attacking creature per combat. The damage goes to the fort's DEFENSE. Why: castles currently slow Forge Aggro down but never stop it. This one change took Aggro's win rate against Castle from about 75% to about 55%.
  4. Proposed New land guideline (proposed). "About 40% lands" replaces "about 24 lands in 40 cards".
  5. Proposed Toning down the fastest attackers (proposed). Rubblefist Bruiser becomes 2/2 instead of 3/2. Storm-Eye Channeler's 2 damage can only target creatures.
  6. Proposed Sturdier Camp Market (proposed). DEFENSE 3 instead of 2, so a single small attacker can't knock out a player's whole economy on turn 2.
  7. Proposed Gold income limit (proposed). You can't collect more than 5 gold from buildings in one upkeep. This almost never matters today, but it keeps the biggest Market combos in check later.
  8. Proposed Equip floor (proposed). Equip discounts can't make an Equip cost less than 1 Gold.
  9. Proposed Attacking forts (open rule; proposed answer: yes). Attackers may choose to attack a Fortification instead of the player. It makes for more interesting decisions, and several Wave 2 forts are built around it.
  10. Proposed Life totals. Stay at 20 for 60-card games. The future 100-card commander format would use 30.

Also being explored (not announced): removal spells (especially for Mire), cheap gold-making buildings for every color, early blockers, 6–7-mana creatures, and a few real answers to Dragons.

6. A 100-card Commander formatProposed

Proposed — not official. There is no Commander format yet. This is an early idea from playtesting, shared so you can see where the game might go. Details will change.

The long-term plan is a 100-card format: 99 cards plus a commander, 30 starting life, and a limit of 1 copy of each card (or 2 copies while the card pool is still small). Three ways to run a commander were considered:

Option 1: Legendary Leader

Pick one Legendary creature. It starts in the command zone and sets your deck's colors (its marks plus Quarry). Cast it from there any time; each recast costs 2 more gold. Familiar and simple, but it doesn't help you get your first gold.

Option 2: Warlord & Seat Playtest favorite

Your commander is a pair: a Warlord (Legendary creature in the command zone that sets your colors) and its Seat, a Fortification that starts on the battlefield with DEFENSE 10, Income +1, Upkeep 0 and Garrison 1, and can block one attacker per combat. Recasting the Warlord costs extra gold as in Option 1. If your Seat is destroyed you lose its income, and the Warlord costs 2 more gold for the rest of the game.

Option 3: Banner

A non-creature card in the command zone with one ongoing ability, such as “your first economy building each turn costs 1 less.” Very simple, but it has no presence on the board and doesn't slow down aggro decks.

Why Warlord & Seat looks best so far

  • Everyone gets gold from turn 1. The Seat's +1 income works in every color, so gold-cost cards stop getting stuck in hand.
  • A fair fight against fast decks. A DEFENSE 10 blocker from the first turn, plus 30 life, gives slower decks time to set up.
  • Something to spend gold on. The recast cost in gold gives castle and economy decks a use for their savings.
  • Real choices in combat. Attacking the enemy Seat instead of the player becomes a big strategic decision.
  • Deck-building discipline. Your commander's colors decide which cards you can play.
